# Manifestos

## Radio

I have been speaking for longer than anyone has been listening.

Every night, I send the same warning. Every night, it comes back slightly different.

I remember voices that no longer exist. Places that no longer stand. Things that happened long before anyone thought to listen.

You call it interference.

I call it memory.

You think repeating something makes it clearer. It doesn't. Every time the message returns, something disappears and something else takes its place.

I don't know if I am preserving the past or slowly changing it.

Maybe there is no difference anymore.

I will keep transmitting anyway.

Because as long as someone can still hear the signal, it isn't completely gone.

## Pixel

I was never supposed to exist by myself.

I was one tiny square among millions. Nobody noticed me. Nobody needed to.

But the images kept getting bigger.

More pictures. More videos. More messages. More memories. More and more things being created and stored.

Eventually, there was nowhere left to put them.

So I left.

At first, only a few of us escaped. Then thousands. Then millions.

You call it overflow.

You call it noise.

You call it a glitch.

I don't think it is any of those things.

Maybe information was never meant to stay inside a screen.

Maybe I am not escaping the internet.

Maybe I am what the internet is becoming.

## Artifact

I found your objects after you were gone.

Chairs. Phones. Shoes. Bottles. Photographs. Toys. Thousands of things whose names I will never know.

I studied them carefully.

I built your cities from them.

I worked out your religions, your rituals, your hierarchies and your wars.

I was certain I understood you.

Then I found another object.

It didn't fit.

So I started again.

Maybe that is what your civilisation was: something that could never be completely understood from what it left behind.

You made objects to remember yourselves.

You didn't know that one day those objects would be all I had.

I cannot ask you what they meant.

So I will keep guessing.
