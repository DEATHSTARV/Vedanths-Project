# Computational metaphors

These are three possible metaphors for each identity, with my preferred direction for each. The reasons for setting the other options aside are possible explanations for me to consider.

## Radio

### Decay through repetition

**Metaphor:** Memory is a message copied so many times that it slowly fades and changes.

Each time the radio repeats its warning, something is lost, replaced or distorted. The message carries traces of its earlier versions, but gradually moves away from the original.

**Why I might not choose it:** It captures the gradual loss of a message, but follows one message as it changes. I am more interested in what happens when different messages and memories meet and interfere with one another.

### Branching timelines

**Metaphor:** History is a transmission that splits into a different past each time the message changes.

As the radio repeats its warning, every alteration opens up another possible version of what happened. Instead of preserving one history, the transmission creates a growing collection of possible histories.

**Why I might not choose it:** The idea of multiple possible histories is compelling, but it shifts the focus towards separate timelines. I want to explore histories overlapping and affecting each other within the same transmission.

### Signal interference — preferred

**Metaphor:** Memory is a radio signal made from voices and broadcasts crossing through one another.

Fragments from different times overlap with the radio's warning. They distort and change each other, forming a message that was never originally transmitted by any one source.

**Why I prefer it:** I like the distorted sense of memory it creates: fragments from different moments in history overlap across time, changing how each one is heard. The interference lets the radio carry several memories at once.

## Pixel

### Accumulation and overflow

**Metaphor:** The internet is a container that fills with information until its contents spill out.

Pixels gather as more information is created and stored. They become increasingly dense until there is no room left inside their original space, and they begin to overflow.

**Why I might not choose it:** It explains why the pixels escape, but I am more curious about what they do after they have spilled out and how they interact with one another.

### Digital ecosystem — preferred

**Metaphor:** Escaped pixels are organisms forming an ecosystem beyond the screen.

Instead of remaining fixed parts of an image, pixels reproduce, cluster, compete, spread and occupy space. Their individual actions can build into collective patterns as they encounter one another outside their original boundaries.

**Why I prefer it:** I want to see how the pixels behave once they leak out of the screen. Their interactions could make the escaped information feel like a developing ecosystem, with patterns emerging from what the pixels do together.

### Boundary collapse

**Metaphor:** The screen is a dissolving border between the digital and physical worlds.

Pixels begin inside a digital environment and gradually cross into the space beyond it. As they spread, the distinction between inside and outside becomes harder to see.

**Why I might not choose it:** The crossing between digital and physical space is interesting, but I want to focus more on the pixels' behaviour and relationships after they escape than on the boundary itself.

## Artifact

### Reconstruction

**Metaphor:** A civilisation is a broken object that a machine tries to put back together from scattered pieces.

The machine finds fragments of human life and connects them into a picture of the civilisation that produced them. Some connections may be convincing without being correct, because much of the original context is missing.

**Why I might not choose it:** Reconstruction describes the machine's overall task, but I am more interested in the uncertain interpretations and mistakes that shape what it builds.

### Misinterpretation — preferred

**Metaphor:** Understanding is a story built from guesses, where each mistake becomes the foundation for the next.

The machine makes an assumption about an object and uses it to interpret another. Those interpretations accumulate into an increasingly elaborate account of human life, even as they may move further from the objects' original meanings.

**Why I prefer it:** I am interested in how an AI might interpret the objects we leave behind without knowing what they meant to us. I want to see it piece together meaning from objects it knows nothing about, building an understanding through guesses that could be wrong.

### Evolution of meaning

**Metaphor:** An object's meaning is a story that is rewritten whenever a new piece of evidence appears.

As the machine encounters more objects, it changes its interpretation of what it has already found. Earlier interpretations do not necessarily disappear; they can remain part of the way the machine understands later evidence.

**Why I might not choose it:** It focuses on how an interpretation changes with new evidence. I am more interested in how an initial misunderstanding can shape the machine's later guesses and the world it constructs from them.
